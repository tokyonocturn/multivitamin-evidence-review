# Evidence review by outcome

This is the narrative companion to
[`data/evidence.csv`](../data/evidence.csv) and
[`docs/generated/evidence-tables.md`](generated/evidence-tables.md). Each section
states what the trials measured, what they found, and how much to trust it.

The short version: multivitamins have been tested in hundreds of thousands of
people for up to 12 years. The results are remarkably consistent. They do not
prevent death, heart attacks or strokes, they produce a small and fragile reduction
in cancer incidence without reducing cancer deaths, they produce a small cognitive
benefit in one large recent trial but not in an older one, and they do not increase
energy.

---

## Mortality

Nine pooled randomised trials (n=51,550) found no reduction in all-cause mortality:
OR 0.94 (95% CI 0.87-1.01). The three largest trials dominate the estimate:
PHS II (HR 0.94, 0.88-1.02), COSMOS (HR 0.93, 0.81-1.08) and SU.VI.MAX (RR 0.77,
0.57-1.00 overall). None was individually significant for mortality.

A 2024 pooled analysis of three prospective US cohorts (n=390,124, 20+ years)
found the opposite direction: daily multivitamin users had a 4% higher mortality
risk (HR 1.04, 1.02-1.07), with consistent results in a time-varying analysis. This
is observational and almost certainly confounded — people with early illness start
supplements — but it rules out a large protective association.

**Verdict:** no mortality benefit. Certainty moderate for the null; the cohort
signal is low certainty and should not be read as proof of harm.

## Cardiovascular disease

PHS II randomised 14,641 male physicians to Centrum Silver or placebo for a median
of 11.2 years. Major cardiovascular events occurred in 745 men on the multivitamin
and 728 on placebo: HR 1.02 (0.92-1.13). There was no effect on MI, stroke or
cardiovascular death. The isolated MI-death finding (HR 0.56) was a subgroup result
with a wide interval and no plausible mechanism.

COSMOS (n=21,442) found a composite CVD HR of 0.98 (0.86-1.12). Pooled CVD
mortality across four trials (n=37,400) was OR 0.94 (0.83-1.06).

**Verdict:** no cardiovascular benefit. Certainty high. This is one of the
best-tested questions in supplement research, and the answer is consistently null.

## Cancer

This is the only domain with a positive randomised signal, and it is smaller than
it looks.

- **Pooled cancer incidence:** OR 0.93 (0.87-0.99), four RCTs, n=48,859. Absolute
  risk difference among adequately powered trials: −0.2% to −1.2%.
- **PHS II:** 17.0 vs 18.3 cancers per 1,000 person-years, HR 0.92 (0.86-0.998),
  P=0.04. The effect was confined to non-prostate cancers (HR 0.88) and to men with
  a baseline cancer history (HR 0.73). Prostate cancer, about half of all events,
  was unaffected.
- **COSMOS:** HR 0.97 (0.86-1.09), null. Lung cancer HR 0.62 (0.42-0.92);
  colorectal HR 1.30 (0.80-2.12); breast HR 1.06 (0.79-1.42).
- **SU.VI.MAX:** overall RR 0.90 (0.76-1.06). Men RR 0.69 (0.53-0.91); women RR
  1.04 (0.85-1.29); interaction P=0.02.
- **Cancer mortality:** OR 0.94 (0.81-1.09), no significant reduction. PHS II
  cancer mortality HR 0.88 (0.77-1.01).

The pooled lung-cancer result (OR 0.75, 0.58-0.95) rests on two trials and an
absolute difference of about 0.2%. It is a hypothesis, not a prevention strategy.

**Verdict:** a small reduction in cancer incidence, no reduction in cancer
mortality, inconsistent across trials and subgroups. Certainty moderate for the
incidence estimate, low for any clinical meaning.

## Cognition

Three COSMOS substudies, using different assessment methods, found a consistent
small benefit in older adults:

| Substudy | n | Assessment | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| COSMOS-Mind | 2,262 | Telephone | Global cognition +0.07 SD (0.02-0.12) |
| COSMOS-Web | 3,562 | Web battery | Immediate recall +0.27 words vs placebo |
| COSMOS-Clinic | 573 | In-person | Global +0.06 SU; episodic memory +0.12 SU |
| Meta-analysis | 5,200 | Mixed | Global +0.07 SU (0.03-0.11); memory +0.06 (0.03-0.10) |

The effect is about 0.07 standard deviations, equivalent to roughly 2 years less
cognitive aging (3.1 years by the COSMOS-Web calibration). It is statistically
robust within COSMOS and consistent across modes of assessment.

Against that, PHS II followed 5,947 men for 12 years and found no effect on global
cognition (−0.01 SU, −0.04 to 0.02) or verbal memory. A 2026 meta-analysis of 10
RCTs (n=13,600) found a small effect on delayed free recall (SMD 0.09) and nothing
else.

**Verdict:** a real but small effect in older adults, not replicated outside
COSMOS, and absent in the longest trial. Certainty moderate for the COSMOS finding,
low for generalisation.

## Immunity and infection

The evidence is weaker than the marketing.

- A systematic review of eight RCTs in older people concluded the evidence was poor
  to moderate quality, heterogeneous and conflicting, and did not support routine
  use in all older people.
- The largest nursing-home trial (n=748, mean age 85) found no overall reduction in
  infections: OR 0.92 (0.82-1.03). A post hoc subgroup without dementia had fewer
  infections (RR 0.81).
- A small trial in adults with type 2 diabetes and subclinical deficiency found
  fewer infection days and less absenteeism (n=130).
- A rapid review of seven RCTs found no reduction in acute respiratory infection
  incidence, possible reduction in some symptoms, and insufficient evidence for
  COVID-19.

**Verdict:** no reliable prevention of infection in well-nourished adults.
Possible benefit in older or subclinically deficient populations, low certainty.

## Energy and fatigue

There is no randomised evidence that a multivitamin improves energy in replete
adults, and a prospective cohort found no association between multivitamin use and
fatigue severity or incidence. B-vitamin supplementation does not improve fatigue,
cognition or depression in people without deficiency.

The mechanism is straightforward: B vitamins are cofactors in energy metabolism.
They are not fuel, and adding more cofactor to a system that already has enough does
not increase flux.

**Verdict:** unsupported. Certainty moderate.

---

## Pregnancy

Folic acid is the strongest evidence in the field. The MRC Vitamin Study randomised
1,817 women with a previous affected pregnancy to 4 mg folic acid or placebo and
found a 72% reduction in neural tube defects. Czeizel and Dudás found a similar
effect for first occurrence with a multivitamin containing 0.8 mg folic acid. The
USPSTF recommends 0.4-0.8 mg/day for all women planning or capable of pregnancy.

Prenatal multis also supply iodine, iron and vitamin D. Observational and
meta-analytic data suggest maternal multivitamin use may reduce paediatric cancer
risk, but confounding is likely and certainty is very low.

**Verdict:** benefit established for folic acid and neural tube defects. Certainty
high.

## Eyes

The AREDS formula (500 mg vitamin C, 400 IU vitamin E, 15 mg beta-carotene, 80 mg
zinc, 2 mg copper) reduced progression from intermediate to advanced AMD by about
25-28% (OR 0.72, 0.58-0.90; about 78 fewer cases per 1,000 over 6 years). AREDS2
replaced beta-carotene with 10 mg lutein and 2 mg zeaxanthin; the primary analysis
was not significant (HR 0.90, 0.76-1.07), but lutein/zeaxanthin was better than
beta-carotene in secondary analyses (HR 0.82, 0.69-0.96), and it is safer for
smokers.

This is not a general multivitamin. The doses are 5-10 times higher, and the
benefit is limited to people with intermediate AMD. There is no benefit for
prevention in people without AMD.

A 2026 rapid review also reported an unexpected higher risk of AMD progression with
multivitamin use. That finding conflicts with AREDS and is very low certainty; it
may reflect formulation differences or confounding. It is a hypothesis, not a
reason to avoid a multivitamin.

**Verdict:** AREDS2 formula benefits intermediate AMD. Certainty moderate. General
multis do not.

## Blood pressure and other outcomes

A rapid review of meta-analyses reported small reductions in systolic blood
pressure with multivitamin use in at-risk populations. The effect is too small and
too inconsistently measured to be a treatment for hypertension.

No consistent benefit has been shown for visual acuity, COVID-19 outcomes,
frailty, biological aging, hair growth or exercise performance.

---

## What the trials cannot tell us

1. **They tested few products.** Centrum Silver accounts for most of the modern
   evidence. Premium capsules, gummies, whole-food tablets and greens powders have
   not been tested for outcomes.
2. **They were short.** Three to twelve years is short for cancer and
   cardiovascular disease.
3. **They were narrow.** Male physicians and mostly white, educated older adults.
4. **They measured the wrong thing for marketing.** "Immune support" and "energy"
   are not endpoints, so trials cannot confirm them.
5. **They cannot rule out small effects.** A 6% mortality reduction would be
   clinically meaningful and is not excluded by the pooled confidence interval.

The correct conclusion is not "multivitamins do nothing". It is "the average effect
on the outcomes that matter is small, and the subgroups that benefit are defined by
nutritional need, not by brand or format".
