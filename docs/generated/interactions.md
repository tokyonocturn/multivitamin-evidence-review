# Interactions

Generated from `data/interactions.csv`. Severity reflects the consequence of missing the interaction, not the likelihood that a standard multivitamin causes it.

## High severity

| Pair | Mechanism | Effect | Management |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Zinc (50 mg/day) + copper | Induced metallothionein binds copper in enterocytes | Copper deficiency, anaemia, neuropathy | Keep zinc below 40 mg/day or add 1-2 mg copper. A 25 mg zinc multi should contain copper. |
| Vitamin K + warfarin | Direct antagonism of VKORC1 | Unstable INR | Keep vitamin K intake stable. Avoid starting or stopping K2 MK-7 without INR monitoring. |
| Preformed vitamin A + isotretinoin/acitretin | Additive retinoid toxicity | Teratogenicity and hepatotoxicity | Avoid supplemental retinol with prescription retinoids. |
| Levothyroxine + calcium/iron/magnesium | Chelation in the gut | Reduced levothyroxine absorption and worse TSH control | Separate by at least 4 hours. |
| Folic acid + methotrexate | Folate antagonism (therapeutic for methotrexate) | Reduced drug effect at high folate doses | Only with oncology guidance. Low-dose folic acid is used deliberately with low-dose methotrexate. |
| Biotin (5 mg+) + streptavidin-biotin lab assays | Competitive interference | False low TSH and false troponin results | Stop high-dose biotin 48-72 hours before tests; tell the laboratory. |
| Zinc/calcium/magnesium + tetracyclines/quinolones | Chelation | Reduced antibiotic absorption | Separate minerals from the antibiotic by 2-6 hours per labelling. |
| Multivitamin + levothyroxine | Calcium and iron chelation | Reduced levothyroxine absorption | Take levothyroxine on an empty stomach, 4 hours apart from the multivitamin. |

## Moderate severity

| Pair | Mechanism | Effect | Management |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Calcium + iron | Competitive divalent cation absorption | Lower iron absorption when taken together | Separate by 2 hours if treating iron deficiency. A combined multivitamin is acceptable when iron status is not the target. |
| Iron (25 mg+) + zinc | Competition at DMT1 and ZIP transporters | Reduced zinc absorption | Separate high-dose iron from zinc; a multivitamin with 18 mg iron and 11-15 mg zinc is a compromise. |
| Vitamin E (400 IU+) + anticoagulants/antiplatelets | Inhibition of vitamin K-dependent carboxylase; platelet effects | Bleeding risk | Standard multivitamin doses (15-30 mg) are not a concern; high-dose E should be avoided. |
| Proton pump inhibitors + vitamin B12 | Reduced gastric acid prevents release of food-bound B12 | B12 deficiency with long-term use | Crystalline B12 in a multivitamin is absorbed without acid; monitor B12 if symptoms or long-term use. |
| Proton pump inhibitors + iron | Reduced acid impairs ferric iron reduction | Lower iron absorption | Use bisglycinate or take with vitamin C; treat deficiency with a dedicated course. |
| Metformin + vitamin B12 | Reduced ileal calcium-dependent absorption | Lower B12 status | Periodic B12 measurement; a multivitamin may prevent but not treat deficiency. |
| Multivitamin (K-containing) + warfarin | Change in vitamin K intake | INR instability | Choose a consistent product or a K-free formula, and tell the anticoagulation service. |
| Multivitamin + standalone vitamin D | Additive dose | Approaching the 4,000 IU UL | Add every source. A 1,000 IU multi plus a 5,000 IU capsule is 6,000 IU. |
| Multivitamin + B-complex | Additive B6 and niacin | B6 above 100 mg or niacin above 35 mg | Check the sum. Two 'high-potency' products can exceed the UL for B6 and niacin. |
| Multivitamin + zinc lozenges/immune tablets | Additive zinc | Copper deficiency and nausea | Short courses of lozenges are acceptable; chronic stacking is not. |
| Multivitamin + iron tablet | Additive iron | GI effects and overload | Only combine with a documented need and monitoring. |

## Low severity

| Pair | Mechanism | Effect | Management |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vitamin C + iron | Ascorbate reduces ferric to ferrous iron | Increased iron absorption | Beneficial in iron deficiency; a concern only in iron overload. |
| Calcium + magnesium (high doses) | Competition at absorption | Modest reduction at high doses | Not clinically important at multivitamin doses. |
| Vitamin C (1 g+) + warfarin | Unclear; possible interference with warfarin metabolism | Reduced anticoagulant effect in case reports | Limit supplemental vitamin C to 1 g/day and monitor INR. |
| Vitamin A (high dose) + warfarin | Additive effect on INR? | Increased bleeding risk in case reports | Avoid high-dose retinol; standard multivitamin doses are not a concern. |
| Iron + tea/coffee polyphenols | Chelation of non-haem iron | Reduced iron absorption | Separate by 1-2 hours when treating deficiency. |
