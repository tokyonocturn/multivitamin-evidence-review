# Upper limits

Adult tolerable upper intake levels used in this review, with the critical effect each is based on.

| Nutrient | UL | Unit | Basis | Critical effect | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vitamin A (preformed) | 3000 | mcg RAE | IOM/EFSA | Teratogenicity and hepatotoxicity | Applies to retinol and retinyl esters. Beta-carotene has no UL but high-dose supplementation is harmful in smokers. |
| Vitamin C | 2000 | mg | IOM | Gastrointestinal upset and oxalate risk | Above 2 g/day diarrhoea is common; stone risk in susceptible people. |
| Vitamin D | 100 | ug | IOM | Hypercalcaemia | 4,000 IU. Toxicity is rare below 10,000 IU/day but has occurred with manufacturing errors. |
| Vitamin E | 1000 | mg | IOM | Haemorrhage | Any form of alpha-tocopherol. High-dose trials found a small mortality signal at 400 IU or more. |
| Niacin (synthetic) | 35 | mg | IOM | Flushing and hepatotoxicity | Applies to nicotinic acid and niacinamide. Gram doses cause liver injury. |
| Vitamin B6 | 100 | mg | IOM | Sensory peripheral neuropathy | Cases reported below 100 mg with prolonged use; several multis supply 50-75 mg. |
| Folic acid | 1000 | ug | IOM | Masking B12 deficiency | Applies to synthetic folic acid; 5-MTHF is not subject to a UL but has not been proven free of masking risk. |
| Choline | 3500 | mg | IOM | Body odour and hypotension | Rarely relevant to multis. |
| Calcium | 2000 | mg | IOM | Hypercalcaemia and GI effects | 2,500 mg for adults 19-50 in the IOM framework; 2,000 mg is the conservative figure used here. |
| Iron | 45 | mg | IOM | GI effects and iron overload | Supplemental iron. People with haemochromatosis absorb excess regardless. |
| Iodine | 1100 | ug | IOM | Thyroid dysfunction | Kelp and prenatal products are the usual culprits. |
| Magnesium | 350 | mg (supplemental) | IOM | Diarrhoea | Applies to supplemental magnesium only, not food. |
| Zinc | 40 | mg | IOM | Copper deficiency and immune impairment | 50 mg/day for weeks lowers copper; 40 mg is the UL. |
| Selenium | 400 | ug | IOM | Selenosis | 200 mcg is 50% of the UL and common in premium multis. |
| Copper | 10 | mg | IOM | Liver toxicity | Rarely approached; the risk is deficiency from excess zinc, not excess copper. |
| Manganese | 11 | mg | IOM | Neurological effects | Several multis supply 2-4 mg without need. |
| Molybdenum | 2000 | ug | IOM | Gout-like syndrome | Not a practical concern at supplement doses. |
| Boron | 20 | mg | EFSA | Reproductive and developmental toxicity | EFSA guidance level; no US UL. |
| Vitamin K | None | not established | IOM | No established toxicity | The risk is interaction with warfarin, not direct toxicity. |
| Vitamin B12 | None | not established | IOM | No established toxicity | No UL because absorption is limited and excess is excreted. |
| Biotin | None | not established | IOM | No established toxicity | The practical issue is laboratory interference, not toxicity. |
| Pantothenic acid | None | not established | IOM | No established toxicity | Diarrhoea at very high doses only. |
